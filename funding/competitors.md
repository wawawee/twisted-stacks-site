                          │

TwistedStacks is nearly alone in the **build-first × AI-native × regional × grant-first** quadrant. Hexa shares build-first/AI-adjacent but is Paris-scale and equity-first; Sting/Movexum share regional/grant-savvy but are **program-first, not builders**; Antler is capital-first. **The white space is real** — and so is the reason it's empty: it demands a tiny team to both *build many things* and *run the grant machine*. That is the thesis and the risk in one sentence.

## 4. Competitive takeaways

1. **TwistedStacks is not competing with Sting/Movexum — it should partner with them.** They are the regional grant/credibility on-ramp, not rivals. Route products *through* Movexum for Region innovationsstöd.
2. **Hexa is the aspirational template and the cautionary tale.** It validates multi-vertical shared-platform compounding — and quantifies the team depth (~30 specialists) required to do it without dropping products.
3. **The differentiated wedge is grant-leverage + AI-native focus in a region no studio occupies.** Sustainable only with disciplined sequencing; otherwise the multi-vertical breadth becomes the weakness, not the moat.
4. **Exit design should assume the Swedish reality:** acquisition or independence, not an IPO moonshot (EQT's own data: ~30% of EU unicorns relocated HQ, mostly to the US).
